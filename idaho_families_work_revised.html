Practical first steps

Four ways to start before making a major financial investment.

The goal is not to solve every family challenge. It is to remove avoidable friction that keeps people from showing up, staying and growing.

Flexibility

Let work fit real life where operations allow — flexible start times, shift swaps with clear guardrails and seasonal flexibility during school breaks or other high-need periods.

30-day test · adjust one scheduling rule

Predictability

Care arrangements depend on knowing work schedules early enough to plan. Post schedules sooner, reduce last-minute changes and keep hours consistent where possible.

30-day test · post one week earlier

Supportive culture

A supervisor’s response can determine whether a family challenge becomes a short-term disruption or a reason to leave. Give managers practical tools to problem-solve consistently.

30-day test · give managers a decision tree

Resource connection

You do not have to solve every problem yourself. Make existing child care and family supports easy for employees to find and use.

30-day test · publish a family resources page
Shared funding

You do not carry the cost alone.

Cost-sharing models can divide child care costs between employers and employees and, in some models, public, philanthropic or community partners — making meaningful support more financially workable.

Duo-Share

Employer and employee share the cost directly, without requiring a third funding partner. Contributions can be structured as a percentage or a defined employer amount.

EMPLOYER EMPLOYEE

Tri-Share

Employer, employee and a public, philanthropic or community partner share the cost. The contribution structure can be tailored to the program and available funding.

EMPLOYER EMPLOYEE PARTNER

Federal tax credit

Enhanced from 2026: eligible employer child care expenditures may qualify for a federal tax credit, with higher credit rates available to qualifying small businesses.

YOUR NET COST CREDIT

Start with the workforce problem, choose the right solution, then work with a tax professional to determine whether the investment qualifies. Idaho Families Work does not provide tax advice.

How we help

From workforce problem to practical action.

We help Idaho employers understand their workforce needs, choose right-fit solutions, put them into practice and measure what changes. Start small. Scale smart. Finance well.

Readiness assessment

A structured diagnostic of your workforce, pain points, current supports and local care landscape — so you know the right place to start before making a major investment.

Solution design

A 30-day action sprint, a pilot concept with cost assumptions and owners, a partner map and a measurement plan. A practical plan, not a best-practices memo.

Implementation, training & measurement

Pilot support, executive briefings and manager workshops to put the plan into practice, followed by measurement of retention, absenteeism, utilization and lessons learned.
